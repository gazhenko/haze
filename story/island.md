## Order of play

Prologue → the Baths (linear; effectively the tutorial) → the Village (hub) → the Springs, the West Cliffs and the Cure in any order → the Summit → the Light → the epilogue.

| Region | Opens when | Depends on | Gives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Baths | The cliff gate is open | — | The north gate; the island’s list of rounds |
| The Village | The Baths are finished | — | The Morse chart and telegram; the tide lesson; the song’s words |
| The Springs | The Village is reached | — | Water pressure for the Cure |
| The West Cliffs | The Village is reached | The school’s tide lesson | A moving tide (caves, causeway); singing buoys; the HOME bell |
| The Cure | The Village is reached | The Springs, for the lift, the baths and the glasshouse heat | The glasshouse key; the dance card |
| The Summit | The Springs, Cliffs and Cure are in morning | The telegram from the Village | Running clocks; the causeway route; a thinned causeway haze |
| The Light | The Summit is finished and the tide is low | The singing buoys | The ending |

The Village stays in evening until the very end, because it is where she left from.

## Prologue: The crossing (2 rooms)

1. **The haze.** You row the last stretch alone. Faint buoy bells play notes you cannot name yet, and a lantern passes the other way. You steer by sound. The envelope is in your journal from the start: green wax, a pressed leaf.
2. **Landing cove.** A boathouse, a tide board stuck at half-flood, a station clock at 7:42, and the Company sign: MORROW BATHS · THE WATER THAT REMEMBERS.
   *Puzzle:* a counterweighted gate at the top of the cliff stair. It teaches E and Q.

## I. The Baths: “The evening I kept” (7 rooms)

Rooms 3–8 are the shipped 0.4.0 bathhouse, with unchanged puzzles. Its five act titles become chapters within Act I.

3. **Entrance hall and keeper’s desk.** The first letter.
4. **West gallery.** The water manifold: the measure of rain.
5. **East gallery.** The gardener’s slate.
6. **Pool and fountain.** The drowned score and the four bells.
7. **Mirror circuit.** The prism, three mirrors and the keeper’s seal.
8. **Mara’s walled garden.** The last letter and the little valve.
9. **North gate** *(new).* Morning reaches only the headland; a wall of haze stands at the treeline. Mara’s seed tins carry the leaf seal from your envelope. Nailed to the gate in Elias’s hand: *Baths · Springs · Mill · Cure · Clock · Light* — the island’s rounds.

## II. The Village: “The last sailing” (6 rooms)

The story’s heart. Roads leave the quay for every region, each veiled in haze until its round is done.

10. **The quay.** An empty berth. The departures board: LAST SAILING · KITTIWAKE · 7.30, with 7.40 chalked over it.
    *Hub; no puzzle.*
11. **Ferry office.** The passenger list: 61 names ticked at the gangway. *M. Vale ✓*. *E. Vale* unticked. The captain’s note: “held 10 min for E.V.”
    *Puzzle:* a ticket safe whose combination comes from the posted timetable.
12. **Post office and telegraph.** The pigeonhole VALE · BATHS is packed with unopened letters in green wax, postmarked at Ardmouth across a year. Ada’s ledger says “refused” again and again.
    *Puzzle:* decode the receiver tape with the Morse chart on the wall: KITTIWAKE IN SAFE 11.10 ALL WELL · M ASKS WILL HE COME.
13. **The Lantern inn.** The farewell party, abandoned mid-evening. The guest book holds every islander’s last line. Mara’s: “Gone ahead. E. will follow when his rounds are done.”
    *Story room; no puzzle.*
14. **Schoolhouse.** The last lesson is still on the blackboard: the song’s words (its lines begin REED, SUN, STAR, REED, MOON), a child’s map titled “Who keeps the island?”, and a tide-table exercise.
    *Puzzle:* the tide exercise, which teaches the rules used at the tide mill and the causeway.
15. **Chapel and evening bell.** A plaque: WHEN THE ROUNDS ARE KEPT, RING THE EVENING OUT. The bell rope is tied off. There is no grave for Mara; a plot reserved for Elias is empty.
    *Optional final moment:* ring the evening out after the light goes out.

## III. The Springs: “The water that remembers” (5 rooms)

16. **Woodland road.** Pressure gauges along the old water main, all reading low, each with Elias’s chalk tick. The haze is thickest here.
17. **Engineer’s house.** The Company’s closure notice, ten years of falling salt readings, and the engineer’s admission that Mara was right.
    *Clue:* the chart of safe fresh-to-sea ratios used in the pump house.
18. **Pump house.** A beam engine draws on three springs and a sea inlet, with a salt meter.
    *Puzzle:* balance the intakes for pressure without salt — Mara’s rule at island scale.
19. **The cistern.** A vaulted underground reservoir in four chambers. Needs the lantern.
    *Puzzle:* fill and drain chambers to cross them and reach the outflow wheel.
20. **The source.** The warm spring itself, steam and mineral terraces. The Company’s carving sits over an older one: WHAT IS KEPT IS NOT LOST. Elias has chalked *not tonight* on the outflow valve.
    *Final act:* open it.

## IV. The West Cliffs: “What the bells were for” (5 rooms)

21. **Foundry yard.** Racks of moulds. Josef’s tuning forks are marked SUN, MOON, REED, STAR, and a fifth, HOME.
22. **Casting hall.** The patterns for the fountain bells, and a last bell, HOME, still in its mould.
    *Puzzle:* tune the buoy bells to the forks. Sand on a vibrating Chladni plate shows the pitch, so it can be solved without sound.
    *Final act:* break the mould.
23. **Tide mill.** The millpond is held at half-flood.
    *Puzzle:* open the sluices in the order the school lesson taught. The tide moves again; low tide opens the caves and, later, the causeway.
24. **Sea caves.** Dark and echoing; open only at low tide. Needs the lantern.
    *Puzzle:* ring a handbell and follow the shortest echo. Ripples in the rock pools show the same answer.
25. **Buoy winch house.** The channel buoys were hauled up and silenced.
    *Puzzle:* lower them in the song’s order so the channel sings again. The finale is navigated by them.

## V. The Cure: “Her other garden” (5 rooms)

26. **Grand lobby.** The hotel register, brass bath tokens (the same discs as on the mechanisms), and a cage lift that will not run without the Springs.
27. **Hydrotherapy hall.** Tiled pools and needle showers.
    *Puzzle:* a patient’s treatment card gives the order and temperature of each bath; set the hot and cold valves to match.
28. **Ballroom.** Set for the farewell dance, with a stopped orchestrion. A dance card: *last waltz — M.V. — reserved E.V.*, never filled in.
    *Puzzle:* repair the punched music roll using the school’s words for the song. It plays the waltz; nothing else happens.
29. **Dr. Calloway’s rooms.** Case files, and the haze lore in a doctor’s plain words. Her file on Elias: *He believes the house will fail if he stops. He does not believe it of himself.*
    *Puzzle:* match patient numbers between the files and the register to find the glasshouse key.
30. **The Winter Garden.** Mara’s glasshouse nursery: a seed library, propagation benches, and an empty pot labelled *cutting — for the mainland*.
    *Puzzle:* restore heat from the Springs and set the roof vents to each bed’s label.
    *Final act:* water the beds. The glasshouse blooms in time-lapse.

## VI. The Summit: “Two minutes” (3 rooms)

31. **Clock tower.** The master clock drives every clock on the island. Elias strapped its pendulum still at 7:42.
    *Puzzle:* restore the clock’s drive, then release the strap. Every clock on Morrow starts at once.
32. **Camera obscura.** A rotating lens projects the live island onto a white table: which regions are in morning, and — at low tide — the safe line across the causeway sands. A map room and a clue in one.
33. **Signal station.** A heliograph aimed at the lighthouse.
    *Puzzle:* flash the telegram from the post office in Morse. Relaying the arrival was this station’s round; finishing it thins the haze over the causeway.

## VII. The Light: “Come out before the rain” (5 rooms)

34. **The causeway.** Exposed at low tide, in fog.
    *Puzzle:* walk from buoy to buoy in the order of Mara’s song. The obscura’s view of the sand bars is the visual alternative. A wrong turn leads back to the last buoy, never into the sea.
35. **Islet landing.** An empty boat slip. The mooring rope is still wet.
36. **Keeper’s cottage.** Twenty years of one man: two chairs, one never sat in; a drawer of replies to Mara, written and never posted. The log’s last entry: *A light in the haze, coming in. So it can be crossed. I leave the last of each round to whoever that is. I’m going across. Open anything that comes for me — I was always late for it.*
37. **Tower stair and clockwork.** The weight-driven clockwork that turns the lens.
    *Puzzle:* stand the lens down in the order on the keeper’s procedure board. Its interlock holds the lamp-room hatch shut until you do.
38. **Lamp room.** The lens still turning for a boat that came in twenty years ago; his chair facing the sea. You open Mara’s letter.
    *Final act:* put out the light. The haze lifts from the whole island in one shot.
    *Hidden alternative:* wind the clockwork instead and stay as keeper.

## Epilogue: “After the rain” (2 rooms)

39. **Morning on Morrow.** The changed island is free to explore. Then the cove, your boat, and for the first time the mainland on the horizon.
40. **The mainland tree.** A hill above a harbour, an old tree of the same kind as Mara’s, a bench. A small boat drawn up on the shingle below. No people. End.
