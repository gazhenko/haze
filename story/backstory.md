# Backstory

This is the canon. The game never states all of it; the player should be able to infer it, and nothing on screen should contradict it.

## What really happened

### The island

Morrow had warm mineral springs. In the 1880s the Morrow Spring Company built the Baths on the southern headland, a sanatorium inland called the Cure, a pier, and a foundry on the west cliffs. It sold its water as “the water that remembers.”

Warm spring water meeting the cold sea keeps the island half-hidden in haze. Sailors called it the island that comes and goes. The islanders had a saying, *the haze keeps what you won’t let go*, and a custom: when you have finished your evening rounds, you ring the evening out.

### The Closing

By the 1950s salt was seeping into the springs. Mara’s rule on the slate — NOTHING FOR THE SEA — was her fight against it. The Company closed the island. The last ferry, the *Kittiwake*, would leave at 7:30 on a September evening, ahead of a storm.

### The last evening

Mara was ready to go. She had packed seeds and a cutting of her tree. She asked Elias to come out before the rain: walk down to the pier with her. He said he would come as soon as he had finished his rounds.

The captain held the boat ten minutes for him and sailed at 7:40. The rain began at 7:42, while Elias was still at the mirrors. When he came out, she was gone — **on the boat, not dead.** The *Kittiwake* reached Ardmouth safely that night. The telegraph tape in the empty post office says so. No one ever read it.

### Twenty years

Elias stayed, the last islander. Mara’s letters came on the mail boat for about a year, each sealed in green wax with a leaf. He never opened one. The haze thickened, and the boats stopped finding the island.

He did not only keep the Baths. He took over everyone’s rounds — the springs, the tide mill, the buoys, the clocks, the light — and every evening he took each one to its last step and stopped. As long as it was still that evening, she was still about to ask him. That is why the island holds.

### Today

Your lantern is the first light to cross the haze in twenty years. Elias sees it from the lighthouse and understands that the haze can be crossed. He leaves the last step of every round “to whoever that is” and rows out. You pass his lantern in the fog in the game’s first minute and do not know it.

The letter you carry is from Mara, now old. She gave it to you because the boats no longer go.

## How the player learns it

| Act | What the player finds | What they now believe or ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prologue | A sealed letter for E. Vale; an island in haze | Someone sent me to a man who is hard to find |
| I. The Baths | “When I came out, she was gone.” At the end, Mara’s seed tins carry the leaf seal on your envelope | She died that evening. *Then who sent this?* |
| II. The Village | Her name ticked on the passenger list; the telegraph tape; her unopened letters | She lived. *He knew. Why didn’t he go?* |
| III–V. Springs, Cliffs, Cure | The dying springs; the doctor’s file; a reserved dance card; an empty pot where her cutting was | He couldn’t let the evening end, because in it she was still asking |
| VI. The Summit | The clocks start. You send the telegram to the lighthouse | The message that never reached him |
| VII. The Light | His cottage, his unsent replies, his last log entry. You open her letter | He has already gone across |
| Epilogue | The mainland; a tree on a hill; a small boat on the shingle below | — |

Three things stay quiet: whether Elias reaches her, who you are to Mara, and what the haze really is.

## The shipped letters, read again

Both letters stay exactly as they are in 0.4.0. Their key lines read one way on a first visit and another once the island is known.

| Line | First reading | Second reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “One evening I left my rounds unfinished.” | A tragedy he could not prevent | The two minutes between the sailing and the rain |
| “On our last evening she asked me to come out before the rain.” | Their last evening together, before her death | Their last evening on Morrow. “Come out” means come down to the boat |
| “When I came out, she was gone.” | She died | The boat had sailed with her on it |
| “I sealed her garden behind the rounds I never finished, and the evening stayed.” | Grief | A choice he went on making every evening for twenty years |
| “A house cannot keep a person, only a place for them.” | Consolation | He knew all along that the only way to keep her was to leave |

## Mara’s last letter

The text is to be written. It should be short. She is well. The cutting she took is taller than the house now. She stopped waiting long ago, but she never stopped asking: *come out before the rain.* By the time the player reads it, he already has.
